Before you choose a tool, decide what the map is for. “Places I’ve visited” can mean three different maps.

## Fill-in, pin or route?

<div class="table-scroll" role="region" aria-label="Three kinds of travel map compared" tabindex="0">

| Kind of map | Shows | Best for | Weak at |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Fill-in map** | Whole countries, provinces or states coloured in | Counting, comparing with friends, a clean poster | Showing exactly where you went inside a large country |
| **Pin map** | A marker on each city or sight | Remembering specific places | Looking tidy once you have many pins close together |
| **Route map** | A line along the path of one trip | Telling the story of a single journey | Summing up a lifetime of travel |

</div>

A fill-in map is the one people usually mean. It answers “how many countries have you been to?” at a glance, and it prints well. Its known flaw is that one afternoon in Vancouver colours all of Canada. If that bothers you, go one level down: a map of provinces or states is far more honest about a big country than a single block of colour. The Canada map here goes one level further: you tick the cities, parks and landmarks you have been to inside each province, and a province you know well is drawn in a deeper colour than one you only passed through.

## Make a fill-in map in four steps

These steps use WorldTripMap, which is free and needs no account. The same order works with any fill-in map maker.

1. **Choose the level.** Use the [world map](/maps/world/) for countries, the [Canada map](/maps/canada/) for provinces and territories, or the [Canadian cities map](/maps/canada-cities/) for cities. You can keep all three; each has its own progress.
2. **Mark where you have been.** Tap or click a place on the map. On a phone, pinch to zoom, or use the list under the map: searching “Lux” is faster than finding Luxembourg with a fingertip.
3. **Add the places you want to go.** Switch the map to *Want to go* and mark your bucket list. It shows as a striped fill so the two never blur together.
4. **Make it a poster or share it.** Choose *Design my poster*, pick one of four designs, add a title, and download a PNG for sharing or a PDF for print. Sizes, paper and bleed are covered in [how to print a travel map poster](/guides/print-a-travel-map-poster/).

Your map saves itself in your browser as you go. To keep a backup or move it to another device, open *Share* and send yourself the link, which contains the map.

## Decide what “visited” means first

You will mark places faster, and trust the result more, if you settle the rule before you start. Some people count a country once they have left the airport. Some want an overnight stay. There is no official answer, so choose one rule and apply it everywhere. [What counts as visiting a country](/guides/what-counts-as-visiting-a-country/) sets out the common rules, and [how many countries there are](/guides/how-many-countries-are-there/) explains why different apps give different totals.

## When a pin map or route map is the better choice

- **You want to remember specific sights.** A pin map is right. Google’s My Maps lets you drop markers and attach notes and photos to each.
- **You are documenting one trip.** A route map tells that story better: a line from stop to stop, in order.
- **You want something for the wall that you update by hand.** A scratch-off map or a cork board with pins does the job with no screen involved.

Many travellers keep two: a fill-in map for the big picture, and a pin or route map for trips they want to remember in detail.

## Tips that save time later

- **Start with the list, not the map.** Go continent by continent and tick as you read. You will remember places you would have missed. The [countries checklist](/guides/countries-visited-checklist/) has all 195 on one page.
- **Check the small ones.** City states and islands are easy to forget: Monaco, the Vatican, Singapore, Malta, the Caribbean.
- **Keep territories separate.** Puerto Rico, Greenland and Hong Kong are worth marking, but they are not on the list of 195 countries. A good map counts them on their own line.
- **Save the link.** A share link is a complete copy of your map. Email it to yourself once in a while.

## Where the map shapes come from

WorldTripMap draws its boundaries from Natural Earth, a public-domain dataset maintained by volunteer cartographers, and projects them with projections chosen for each map rather than the stretched Mercator view of most web maps. The details are on the [data and methods page](/methodology/).
