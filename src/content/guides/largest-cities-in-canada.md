**Canada’s largest city is Toronto, with 6,202,225 people in its metropolitan area at the 2021 Census. Montréal is second with 4,291,732 and Vancouver third with 2,642,825.** Six metropolitan areas have more than a million people: those three, then Ottawa–Gatineau, Calgary and Edmonton.

The table ranks all 41 of Canada’s census metropolitan areas. They are the same 41 you can mark on the [Canadian cities map](/maps/canada-cities/), which adds four smaller capitals so that every capital is on it.

Together the 41 had 27,281,119 people in 2021. Statistics Canada put it this way: nearly three in four Canadians, 73.7%, lived in one of the country’s large urban centres.

## What counts as a city here

Each row is a **census metropolitan area**, the unit Statistics Canada uses for a city and the places that work as part of it. In its definition, a metropolitan area is “formed by one or more adjacent municipalities centred on a population centre”, and must have at least 100,000 people, of whom 50,000 or more live in the core.

So “Toronto” in this table is the City of Toronto together with neighbours such as Mississauga, Brampton, Markham and Vaughan. Hamilton and Oshawa, close as they are, are metropolitan areas of their own and have their own rows.

That is the main reason lists disagree. A ranking by municipality counts only the people inside each city’s own limits, and city limits are drawn very differently from one place to the next. A ranking by metropolitan area, like this one, compares like with like.

Six of the 41 were new in 2021, having grown past the threshold: Fredericton, Drummondville, Red Deer, Kamloops, Chilliwack and Nanaimo.

Ontario has 16 of the 41. Prince Edward Island and the three territories have none: Charlottetown, Whitehorse, Yellowknife and Iqaluit are capitals, but none is a census metropolitan area. They are on the cities map all the same.

## The capital is often not the largest city

- **Canada:** the capital region, Ottawa–Gatineau, is fourth.
- **Alberta:** Calgary (1,481,806) is larger than the capital, Edmonton (1,418,118).
- **British Columbia:** Vancouver is more than six times the size of the capital, Victoria (397,237).
- **Quebec:** Montréal is five times the size of the capital, Québec City (839,311).
- **Saskatchewan:** Saskatoon (317,480) is larger than the capital, Regina (249,217).
- **New Brunswick:** the capital, Fredericton (108,610), is the smallest of the province’s three metropolitan areas, behind Moncton (157,717) and Saint John (130,613).

## Have the numbers changed since 2021?

Yes. Cities have grown, and Statistics Canada publishes estimates between censuses. Its estimate for 1 July 2025 was 31,169,100 people in the 41 metropolitan areas, or 74.8% of Canadians. From 2024 to 2025 the fastest growth was in Edmonton (up 3.0%), Moncton (2.9%) and Calgary (2.9%), while Toronto’s population was virtually unchanged.

Those estimates start from the 2021 Census, which is why the ranking above uses it: it is the most recent full count. A new census was taken in 2026, and Statistics Canada has scheduled its first results, the population and dwelling counts, for 10 February 2027.

## From a list to a map

A ranking tells you which cities are big. It does not tell you which ones you have seen. On the [cities map](/maps/canada-cities/) you mark the ones you have been to and get a count out of 45. On the [Canada map](/maps/canada/) the same cities sit inside their provinces, next to national parks and landmarks, and each province gets its own percentage. For the provinces themselves, see [Canada’s 10 provinces and 3 territories](/guides/canada-provinces-and-territories/).
