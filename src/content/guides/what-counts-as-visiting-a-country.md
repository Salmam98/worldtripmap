**There is no official definition of visiting a country, so you choose a rule and apply it to every country.** Two well-known travellers’ clubs show how far apart reasonable rules can be. The Travelers’ Century Club accepts “even the shortest visit”, including “a plane fuel stop”, as long as you get off and set foot there. NomadMania rejects airport visits outright.

A common middle ground sits between the two: **a country counts once you have left the airport, port or station and done something there.**

## Does an airport layover count?

It depends on the rulebook.

- **Travelers’ Century Club: yes, if you got off the plane.** Its rule is that you “disembark from your aircraft, ship, or vehicle and set foot in the territory”. It decided that “even the shortest visit would suffice”, “even if only a port-of-call, or a plane fuel stop”.
- **NomadMania: no.** Its rules say that visits “involving airports are rejected as a minimum visit to a country”. In the poll it reports, passing through without crossing immigration was rejected by 95%, and crossing immigration but staying in the airport area by 85%. A night in a hotel outside the airport area is accepted.

If you want a number other travellers will not argue with, do not count airports.

## The same trips under two rulebooks

This table is our reading of the two published rules. Where a club gives a ruling in its own words, they are quoted.

| Changing planes without leaving the airport | Counts, if you left the aircraft and set foot there | Does not count |

| Crossing by train without getting off | Does not count: you have not set foot there | Does not count: it “can never be considered a minimal visit” |
| Driving through without stopping | Does not count unless you got out | Does not count |
| A business trip where you saw nothing else | Counts | Counts: “still a minimal visit” |

## Four rules people use

From the loosest to the strictest:

1. **You set foot there.** The Travelers’ Century Club rule. Layovers count if you left the plane.
2. **You left the airport, port or station.** The usual middle ground.
3. **You spent a night.**
4. **You did something there** that you would tell a friend about.

Pick the strictest rule you will actually keep to. A stricter rule gives a smaller number that needs no explaining.

## Awkward cases, and a workable answer for each

These are our suggestions, not anyone’s rulebook.

- **Countries that have changed.** If you went to the Soviet Union, Yugoslavia or Czechoslovakia, count the country that the places you saw are in today.
- **Territories.** Greenland, Puerto Rico and Hong Kong are worth marking, on a line of their own. See [Is Greenland a country?](/guides/what-counts-as-a-country/)
- **Where you live, and where you were born.** They count.
- **Flying over, or seeing it from a ship.** Neither counts under any rule here. NomadMania says it plainly: “sensing from a vessel alone is not accepted as a visit”.
- **Asleep on a night train.** NomadMania rules it out, and it would fail rule 2 in any case.
- **The same country twice.** A country is counted once, however often you go back.

## What WorldTripMap does

The [world map](/maps/world/) does not impose a rule. You mark what you count, as explored or as somewhere you want to go, and the total is always out of the same 195. Agree the rule with anyone you plan to compare maps with, and the comparison will be fair.

One limit no rule can fix: a tick on a country says you were there, not how much of it you saw. For Canada you can go a level deeper and tick cities, parks and landmarks inside each province on the [Canada map](/maps/canada/).
