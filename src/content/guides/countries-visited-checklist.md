**This is a checklist of all 195 countries: the 193 member states of the United Nations and its two observer states, the Holy See (Vatican City) and Palestine.** They are grouped by continent, in alphabetical order, with a box beside each.

There are three ways to use it:

- **On paper.** Print this page, which is laid out for paper with the menus left off, or use the one-page PDF.
- **On screen.** Tick the countries on the [world map](/maps/world/). It keeps the count and the percentage, saves in your browser, and turns the result into a poster. [How to make a map of places you’ve visited](/guides/how-to-make-a-map-of-places-youve-visited/) walks through it in four steps.
- **As a reference** for how many countries each continent has.

## Where the continents are drawn

The continents here follow the United Nations’ statistical regions, with the Americas split in two. That decides a few cases people argue about:

- **Russia** is under Europe. **Kazakhstan**, **Türkiye**, **Cyprus**, **Armenia**, **Azerbaijan** and **Georgia** are under Asia.
- **Egypt** is under Africa.
- **Central America and the Caribbean** are under North America, which is why it has 23 countries.

Other lists draw these lines differently. That changes the count for a continent, never the total of 195.

## Before you start ticking

Two decisions make your count mean something.

1. **What counts as a visit.** A layover, a night, a day trip across a border? There is no official rule, so pick one and keep to it. The options are in [what counts as visiting a country](/guides/what-counts-as-visiting-a-country/).
2. **Which list.** This one has 195. Others have 193, 211 or 249, for reasons explained in [how many countries there are](/guides/how-many-countries-are-there/).

When you have your number, [turn it into a percentage](/guides/what-percentage-of-the-world-have-you-visited/): it is your count divided by 195.
