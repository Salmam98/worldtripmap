# Keyword research and keyword map

What people type when they want what WorldTripMap offers, who answers those searches today, and
which page of the site is meant to answer each one. The first round was collected on
**10 October 2026** (Toronto time), before the site had any visitors. A second round, for the seven
guides added on **11 October 2026**, is marked with that date wherever it appears (A33 to A44).

## Read this first: what the research can and cannot tell you

**No search volumes, difficulty scores, costs per click, trend lines or ranking forecasts appear
in this document.** None were available: no paid keyword tool was used, and a site that is not
live has no Search Console data. Rather than estimate, the columns are left out. Where a priority
is given, it is a judgement from the evidence listed beside it, and the reasoning is written down
so you can disagree with it.

What was used instead:

| Code | Evidence | What it shows | What it does not show |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | Google Autocomplete suggestions for 44 starting phrases (English, Canada): A1 to A32 on 10 October, A33 to A44 on 11 October | The exact wording Google offers as people type, so real phrasings | How many people search each one |
| **S** | The first page of results for 16 searches | Who answers the search today, and what kind of page wins | Positions on Google itself. The results came through a research search tool, are not personalised, and can differ by country and device |
| **P** | Four competing pages read closely | What existing tools do and leave out | Their traffic |
| **G** | Google's and OpenAI's own documentation | The rules the plan has to follow | Anything about rankings |

The raw data is in the appendices, so every claim can be checked.

**How to add real numbers after launch**

1. **Search Console, Performance report** (free, the best source): the actual queries that show
   the site, with impressions and clicks. Useful from about four weeks after launch.
2. **Google Ads Keyword Planner** (free with an Ads account): monthly search ranges for the
   phrases in the keyword map. Record the date and the location setting when you add them.
3. **Google Trends** (free): relative interest over time and by region, useful for seasonality.

When you have them, add a column to the keyword map. Do not back-fill guesses.

## What people type

Autocomplete lists are in Appendix A. The patterns that matter:

**1. Visited-countries searches are tool searches.** After "visited countries map" Google offers
*free, percentage, europe, app, generator, online, with names, gift* (A1). After "countries
visited": *map, checklist, counter, tracker, app, list* (A18). After "world map countries
visited": *checklist, app, color, mark, interactive, count, clickable* (A22). After "how many
countries have i visited": *counter, list, checklist, map, quiz, app, tracker, calculator* (A12).
People want something to click and a number at the end. They also ask for the share of the world
outright: "what percentage of the world have i been to / visited / seen / travelled" (A19).

**2. The Canada phrasing exists, and it is specific.** "visited provinces map" leads to "canada
visited provinces map" and "visited states and provinces map" (A4). "canada provinces visited"
leads to "canada provinces visited map" (A5). "canada travel map" leads first to "canada travel
map fill in" (A23).

**3. Canada's geography questions come with a fixed set of add-ons.** "how many provinces in
canada" is completed with *and territories, and their names, and their capitals, map, and what are
they* (A10). "provinces and territories of canada" with *and capitals, quiz, map, flags, by
population* (A24). "map of canada provinces" with *and territories, and capitals, and cities,
blank* (A20). A page that answers "how many" should give names, capitals and a map in the same
place.

**4. Poster and gift searches are mostly about physical products.** "personalized travel map" is
completed with *with pins, with push pins, for couples, gifts, art, wedding gift* (A7); "travel map
gift" with *for couple, ideas, for him, with pins* (A25). Two phrasings point the other way,
toward something made on a screen: "personal travel map online" (A7) and "scratch off map app",
"scratch off map online" (A13). "map poster maker" is completed with *free, generator, custom,
traveller map poster maker, city map poster maker* (A21).

**5. "travel map poster" alone is a weak phrase for this product.** Its suggestions drift to "road
map poster", "journey map poster", "road map poster design / ideas / drawing" (A6), which reads as
school projects and design inspiration.

**6. City tracking has little search phrasing behind it.** "canadian cities visited" returned only
three suggestions, none about tracking: "canada visit cities", "which canadian city should i
visit", "largest canadian cities" (A14).

**7. Printing questions are practical and local.** "how to print a poster" is completed with *on
multiple pages, at staples, at home, on canva* (A26); "poster size for printing" with *inches,
standard, canva, best* (A15).

**8. The planned maps face different intents.** "visited states map" has the same tool modifiers
as the countries search: *generator, interactive, app, free, online, counter* (A11). "bangladesh
64 districts map" does not: its suggestions are *pdf, hd, png, bangla, name, road map, 4k, pdf
download* (A16). People typing that want a reference map file, not a tracker.

**9. People look for lists to tick off inside a country.** "national parks of canada checklist"
leads to "national parks visited checklist" and "national parks checklist" (A32). "ontario bucket
list" is completed with *summer, parks, winter, fall, things to do in ontario* (A30). "places to
visit in ontario" is planning, not tracking: *in summer, for the weekend, with kids, day trip*
(A31). The places inside each province on the Canada map answer the first two; the third is a
different job.

**10. Some "places I've been" searches are about phone location history.** "map of where i ve been
today" and "map of where i ve been iphone" (A3) are not this product, so pages aimed at that
family should say plainly what kind of map this is.

What follows for WorldTripMap, stated as differences that are true of the built site:

- It says what it counts (195, with the rule) and shows a count of places separately from any
  share of area. The percentage is explained on the Data and methods page.
- It has a Canada map with all 13 provinces and territories as its own page.
- It makes a poster file, PNG or vector PDF, from the same marks.
- It needs no account and no install.
- On the Canada map it goes one level deeper: places ticked inside each province give a
  percentage for that province and for Canada, with the list and the formula published.

These are reasons a visitor might prefer it. They are not predictions about rankings.

## Decisions taken from the evidence

1. **One page per intent.** "free", "online", "generator", "interactive", "checklist", "counter"
   and "tracker" are modifiers of one search, so they are answered in the copy of one page, not
   with a page each.
2. **Page titles use the searched wording.** "Visited Countries Map" and "Visited Provinces Map
   of Canada" open the titles of the two main tools; the on-page voice stays "explored".
3. **The Canada map gets first-priority attention**, because the phrase exists (A4, A5) and no
   dedicated tool appeared in the samples (S3, S4, S5).
4. **The poster pages are aimed at "maker" phrasing**, not at "travel map poster" alone (A6) or
   gift searches (A7, A25), which belong to shops.
5. **Guides answer the question in the first paragraph**, then give names, capitals, a table and a
   map, because that is the set of add-ons people type (A10, A20, A24).
6. **No FAQ structured data.** Google's documentation log records that the FAQ rich result "will
   no longer appear in Google Search starting May 7, 2026" (G2). The FAQ sections remain as
   visible content for readers.
7. **Nothing special for AI answers.** Google states there are "no additional requirements to
   appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode" and that no new machine-readable files or special markup are
   needed (G1). The site has no `llms.txt` and no schema added for that purpose.
8. **Second round, 11 October: questions with one right answer get a guide each.** The new lists
   are dominated by questions that have a checkable answer: *how many unesco world heritage sites
   in canada*, *canada's first unesco world heritage site* (A33), *which province has the most
   national parks in canada* (A40), *largest cities in canada by population / in order* (A35),
   *is greenland a country or part of denmark* (A36), *does going to an airport count as visiting
   a country* (A39). Each became one guide that gives the answer in its first sentence, shows the
   full list from the site's own data, and names the official source. Puerto Rico and Hong Kong
   (A37, A38) are answered inside the Greenland guide, under their own headings, because the
   answer has the same shape and three thin pages would serve nobody.
9. **Where a list is the product, there is a file.** "checklist pdf" and "checklist free" follow
   "countries visited checklist" (A27), so the two checklist guides come with a one-page PDF in
   Letter and A4, made from the same data as the page and the map.

## Keeping this current

- Re-run the autocomplete check twice a year. Each list in Appendix A came from
  `https://suggestqueries.google.com/complete/search?client=firefox&hl=en&gl=ca&q=` followed by
  the phrase, with spaces written as `+`. This is the address browsers use for search
  suggestions. It is not a documented Google service and could change; typing the phrase into
  Google in a private window shows the same kind of list.
- After four weeks live, export the Search Console queries and compare them with the keyword map.
  Add any query that shows a page you did not plan for; fix the page's title if the wording
  people use differs from yours.
- Add volumes only from a named tool, with the date and location.
